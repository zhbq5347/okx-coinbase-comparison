### Maker vs taker fees

A **maker order** adds liquidity to the order book. A limit order that sits on the book may qualify as a maker order if it is not filled immediately.

A **taker order** removes liquidity. Market orders and limit orders that execute immediately are usually charged at the taker rate.

Coinbase explains that a partially filled order can include both maker and taker portions. OKX similarly calculates fees according to how the order interacts with the book.

For a casual user making occasional purchases, the maker-taker difference may not be the first concern. For someone trading every week, however, a fee gap of several tenths of a percent can become noticeable quickly.

## Security and custody

Both platforms publish security-related information, but the disclosures are not identical.

OKX publishes proof-of-reserves reports and says it maintains a 1:1 reserve ratio for supported account assets. Its public system includes wallet-address information, liability reports, and zk-STARK-based verification files. The latest report page available at the time of checking showed a September 2026 report and coverage for assets including BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC, XRP, DOGE, SOL, and OKB.

Proof of reserves is useful because it can show whether an exchange held enough identified assets at a particular snapshot. It does not eliminate every custodial, operational, regulatory, smart-contract, or market risk.

Coinbase says its customer assets are held 1:1 and that assets are not lent without customer consent on its Coinbase Advanced product page. Coinbase also operates as a publicly traded company and publishes financial and legal disclosures. Those facts may matter to users who place a high value on corporate reporting and a US-centered compliance structure.

Neither platform should be treated like a bank account. Crypto held on an exchange can be exposed to account compromise, withdrawal restrictions, operational failures, market volatility, and changes in local regulation. Strong account security remains necessary:

- Use a unique password.
- Enable an authenticator app or hardware security key.
- Set withdrawal allowlists where available.
- Confirm the network before sending crypto.
- Avoid leaving large balances on an exchange without a clear reason.
